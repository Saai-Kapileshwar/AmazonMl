[0.0-3.2]  Welcome to the Amazon ML Challenge 2026.
[3.2-6.5]  This year's problem is business entity resolution,
[6.5-8.9]  a fundamental and widely encountered problem
[8.9-10.4]  in real world data.
[10.4-12.0]  You will be given business records
[12.0-14.4]  that arrive from three independent sources,
[14.4-16.6]  each noisy and inconsistent.
[16.6-18.8]  And the goal is to determine which of them
[18.8-21.1]  describe the same real world business.
[21.1-24.2]  In this video, I will walk you through the problem statement,
[24.2-27.5]  the data set, the scoring, the submission format,
[27.5-29.8]  and how entries are judged.
[29.8-32.2]  Let's start with where this data comes from.
[32.2-35.3]  Consider a business signing up on Amazon Business.
[35.3-37.7]  At signup, we capture its core details,
[37.7-40.0]  such as the business name and address.
[40.0-42.5]  To build a richer picture of that same business,
[42.5-45.8]  we pull in additional information from other data providers.
[45.8-47.6]  Each of these sources typically comes
[47.6-50.3]  from a different data vendor with its own formats
[50.3-51.5]  and conventions.
[51.5-53.8]  The difficulty is that these external sources
[53.8-56.2]  share no common identifier with ours.
[56.2-58.0]  So the only fields we can rely on
[58.0-60.0]  are the business name and address.
[60.0-62.4]  For this challenge, we have deliberately limited
[62.4-65.0]  the data to names and addresses.
[65.0-66.8]  Here is the core difficulty.
[66.8-69.4]  The same real world business is described differently
[69.4-70.6]  by each source.
[70.6-73.7]  One vendor may write Acme Robotics Incorporated,
[73.7-75.6]  another abbreviates the address,
[75.6-78.3]  and a third references a nearby landmark.
[78.3-81.6]  Source one is our clean, deduplicated reference list.
[81.6-84.2]  Sources two and three are the noisy fragments
[84.2-86.3]  that must be reconciled against it.
[86.3-89.6]  And there is no shared identifier linking them.
[89.6-92.7]  Entity resolution is the task of linking these records
[92.7-95.7]  together, establishing that differently written records
[95.7-97.8]  all refer to the same business.
[97.8-99.7]  For every entity in Source one,
[99.7-102.2]  the goal is to find all of its matching records
[102.2-104.0]  in Sources two and three.
[104.0-106.8]  A Source one entity may match many records,
[106.8-109.5]  exactly one, or none at all.
[109.5-111.4]  Comparing every Source one record
[111.4-114.2]  against every Source two and Source three record
[114.2-116.4]  would be far too expensive at scale.
[116.4-118.2]  So we first apply blocking,
[118.2-120.8]  sorting records into buckets using a cheap key
[120.8-123.2]  built from both the name and the address
[123.2-126.3]  so that records likely to match land in the same bucket.
[126.3-128.6]  Each color here is one such block.
[128.6-131.0]  Notice that every record carries its own ID,
[131.0-133.1]  a noisy name and an address,
[133.1-135.7]  and records can group either through a similar name
[135.7-137.2]  or through a shared address.
[137.2-140.8]  We follow the orange block, built around Acme Robotics,
[140.8-143.1]  and the records in it become the candidate matches
[143.1-144.2]  for that entity.
[144.2-146.0]  Blocking favors recall,
[146.0-148.7]  so the bucket also pulls in lookalikes,
[148.7-151.4]  a business with a similar name at a different address,
[151.4-154.2]  and a different business that happens to share an address.
[154.2-157.0]  The matching model removes those in the next step,
[157.0-158.9]  blocking narrows an enormous number
[158.9-161.8]  of possible comparisons down to a manageable set
[161.8-163.8]  of candidate pairs.
[163.8-167.2]  Finally, a matching model scores each candidate pair
[167.2-170.4]  and keeps only the true matches, discarding the rest.
[170.4-173.2]  That produces the two outputs of this challenge,
[173.2-175.6]  the candidate pairs from your blocking stage
[175.6-177.6]  and the final matching results,
[177.6-180.6]  which is the file scored on the leaderboard.
[180.6-182.9]  You are provided with two data sets.

[198.7-201.0]  and this is what you generate predictions for.
